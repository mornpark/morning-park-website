Straight talk · from the counter

Things every driver should know. Including a few most shops don't bring up.

Your rights at a California repair shop, how smog checks really work, and what to ask before anyone touches your car. Plain words, and every point links to the official source.

01

          

            
You get a written estimate before any work. And the shop can't go over it without your OK.

            
In California, a repair shop has to give you a written estimate before it starts. If the job turns out bigger, the shop must call you, explain the extra work and the cost, and get your permission before going on.

            
The fine printYes, even for “just a quick thing.” Especially for “just a quick thing.”

            
BAR consumer guide

          

        

        

          
02

          

            
“Shop supplies” isn't supposed to be its own line on your bill.

            
California doesn't allow shops to bill separately for things generically called shop supplies or miscellaneous parts. What you pay for should be listed as real parts and real labor.

            
The fine printShop rags aren't a line item. They're a lifestyle.

            
BAR invoice rules, CCR §3356(g)

          

        

        

          
03

          

            
You can ask for your old parts back. You just have to ask before the work starts.

            
Tell the shop when you sign the estimate or work order, and it has to return the replaced parts. Parts that go back to the manufacturer under warranty are the exception, and the shop has to offer to show them to you instead.

            
The fine printWhat you do with a used brake pad is between you and your mantel.

            
Business & Professions Code §9884.10BAR consumer guide

          

        

        

          
04

          

            
You don't have to use the dealer to keep your new-car warranty.

            
Routine maintenance and repairs can be done by an independent shop. It's illegal for a dealer to deny warranty coverage just because someone else did the work. Keep your receipts.

            
The fine printKeep the receipts. That's the whole trick. There is no second trick.

            
Federal Trade Commission

          

        

        

          
05

          

            
A free code read is a code, not a diagnosis.

            
A trouble code tells you which system noticed a problem, not which part failed. Finding the cause takes testing. That's the difference between replacing the right part and replacing parts until the light goes off.

            
The fine printThe parts store isn't wrong. It just stops right where it gets interesting.

            
From the shop floor

          

        

        

          
06

          

            
Don't clear your check-engine light or disconnect the battery right before a smog check.

            
Your car runs self-tests called readiness monitors, and they reset when codes are cleared or the battery is disconnected. They need normal driving to finish. Since October 1, 2025, California's rules say all monitors that can be set must be set to pass, and BAR is phasing that in. Drive normally for a while after any repair or battery change before you test.

            
The fine printYour car has to study for this test. Cramming the night before doesn't work.

            
BAR: readiness monitor rules

          

        

        

          
07

          

            
A check-engine light on means an automatic smog fail.

            
In California, a car won't pass a Smog Check with the check-engine light on. Get it diagnosed and fixed first. A passing certificate goes to the DMV electronically and is good for 90 days.

            
The fine printElectrical tape over the light has been tried. It has never worked.

            
BAR Smog Check program

          

        

        

          
08

          

            
Shops don't have to guarantee their work. So ask what the guarantee is, in writing.

            
California doesn't require a repair warranty, but many shops offer one. A real guarantee says who backs it, what it covers, for how long, what you have to do to use it, and what's excluded.

            
The fine printA guarantee you can't find on paper is a mood, not a warranty.

            
BAR consumer guide

          

        

        

          
09

          

            
Ask if the estimate costs anything, and whether a teardown will.

            
Some shops charge for estimates. For complex jobs like transmissions, the shop may have to take things apart to give an accurate estimate, and that teardown can cost money even if you don't go ahead with the repair. Asking up front is normal.

            
The fine printNobody likes surprise math. Including mechanics.

            
BAR consumer guide

          

        

        

          
10

          

            
Getting a second opinion is normal. Good shops expect it.

            
Once you have a written estimate, you're free to take it to another shop for a second look. A big repair should never be a guessing game or a rush decision.

            
The fine printGo ahead. We've been on Grass Valley Highway since 1986. We're not going anywhere.

            
BAR consumer guide
