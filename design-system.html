First-principle framing: Every decision answers one question — does it create clear hierarchy, build flow, and earn trust from a trades business owner who values directness over polish?
The 4 design principles
01 · Hierarchy
Weight creates order.
PP Woodland 700 UPPERCASE = loudest moment. PP Woodland 400 = character. DM Sans 700 = wayfinding. DM Sans 300 = reading. Never the same weight at the same size in the same section.
02 · Contrast & Release
Tension, then relief.
PP Woodland 700 Bold for ALL display text (hero, section heads, monogram, nav logo). DM Sans 300 for body copy releases the tension. Dark moss and bright white sections alternate — never two dark or two white sections back to back.
03 · Restraint
Cobalt fires once per section. That's it.
One filled cobalt CTA per section. Everything else is ghost, text-only, or fern. When cobalt appears only on the action that matters, it becomes a visual beacon — the eye goes there automatically. If every label, button, and eyebrow is cobalt, none of them are.
04 · Flow
One thing leads to the next.
Hero → proof → demo → consult CTA. Each section answers the question the last one raised. Every CTA is one clear ask — never two competing actions on the same screen.
Borrowed composition rules — locked June 2026
From Stripe — Trust
One filled CTA per section. One.
The only filled cobalt button per section is the primary ask. Secondary actions use ghost or text-only. No competing equal-weight buttons on the same screen.
From Linear — Signal
Cobalt is a beacon, not a wallpaper.
Cobalt appears once per section — on the CTA only. Labels, eyebrows, and secondary text drop to moss, fern, or gray. The moment the eye lands on cobalt, it knows what to do.
From Vercel — Precision
Tighten display letter-spacing. Engineered, not typeset.
PP Woodland Elegant layer (400 sentence case) tracks at -0.03em. Statement layer (700 uppercase) pulls back from 0.04em to 0.02em. Tight tracking at display sizes signals intentionality — someone who knows exactly what they're doing.
From Oxide — Identity
Put the credential in the hero. One line.
Above or below the tagline: "Licensed Contractor Converts to Building With Software Instead of With Construction Tools." — signals that JP is the real thing before the headline lands. Differentiator, not bio copy.
